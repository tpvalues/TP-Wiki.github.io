Definitions

From Third Position Wiki

Third Position ▶
Third Position is a wide range of ideologies seeking to transcend both Capitalism and Marxism, in reference to the political and economic divisions of Cold War era Europe. Philosophically, it is grounded in Idealism, often combined with various other forms of metaphysics. Economically, it seeks class collaboration through non-Marxist Socialist or other common ownership economic systems, combined with a Nationalist and Interventionist state.
Fascism ▶
Fascism is an ideology espousing a mix of National Syndicalism fused with Giovanni Gentile’s philosophy of Actual Idealism and rooted in the classical liberalism of Giuseppe Mazzini. Philosophically, it seeks an organic state led by exemplary men, alongside the spiritual revitalization of both the nation and man himself. Economically, it seeks to subordinate both capital and labor to the higher interests of the nation, replacing class conflict with class collaboration organized through worker and employer syndicates.
Socialism ▶
Socialism is an economic and political philosophy advocating social ownership of the means of production, based on the principle that society should ensure an equitable share of national wealth and access to essential needs such as healthcare, education, and housing. It would serve as a foundation for later adaptations, varying in their approach to nationalism, ownership and control of productive assets, and social and economic organization.
National Syndicalism ▶
National Syndicalism is a nationalist adaptation of Revolutionary Syndicalism, emerging from the combination of the syndicalist teachings of Georges Sorel and Charles Maurras’s nationalist thought. The term and synthesis were first developed by Georges Valois and his Cercle Proudhon. It rejects liberalism, Marxism, internationalism, and bourgeois political values, while advocating a form of socialism based on class collaboration through worker syndicates and the organization of society along national rather than international lines.
Corporatism ▶
Corporatism is a political and economic system in which organized groups representing different social and economic interests cooperate and negotiate in pursuit of national interests. The term derives from the Latin corpus, meaning “body.” Third Position Corporatism envisions an authoritarian state in which the economy is organized and coordinated through the participation of employers, workers, and state officials. Its various forms draw inspiration from Revolutionary Syndicalism and Roman Catholic social teachings.
